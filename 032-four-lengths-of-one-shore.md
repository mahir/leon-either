# No. 032 Four Lengths of One Shore

## Image 1 (main shot)

```
Documentary aerial photograph taken straight down from a drone about 40 metres up, of a small rocky cove on a wild northern coast on an overcast morning. Dark grey and black rocks with patches of yellow lichen and brown seaweed fill the lower half of the frame; a narrow curving strip of pale sand sits between them; the upper part of the frame is calm grey green sea with thin white foam where it meets the shore. One single continuous thin bright orange cord is laid exactly along the waterline, entering at the left edge of the frame and leaving at the right edge, following every small bend in and out around every rock and inlet. It is one unbroken line, the only bright colour in the picture. Soft even light, no hard shadows, natural muted colours, crisp contemporary detail. No people, no boats, no buildings. ## Image 2

```
Documentary close up photograph at ground level on a rocky shore on an overcast day. A bare adult hand, seen from the side, presses a thin bright orange cord down into a narrow crack in a wet dark grey rock, the cord bending tightly into the crack. Thin white sea foam washes up and just touches the fingertips. On the flat rock beside the hand lies one short pale wooden ruler, about 10 centimetres long, with plain black tick marks and no numbers. Wet stone glistens, small barnacles on the rock, shallow depth of field with the sea soft and grey behind. Natural muted colours, crisp contemporary photograph. Only one hand in frame, no face.
