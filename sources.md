# Sources

Reference list for Leon Either's works. Read it before writing a new piece. Draw on the ideas and forms here; never copy a work, a title or a well-known line.

How sure each entry is:

- **[checked]**: a search on 29 or 30 Oct 2026 turned up a museum, archive or reference page for this exact work or text.
- **[known]**: well established in the literature, stated from general knowledge, not checked line by line.
- **[verify]**: I believe it is right but a detail (date, wording, first publication) should be checked before it is cited on a page.

Cite only [checked] or [known] items by name in a work's text. Check a [verify] item before citing it, then change its mark.

For much deeper notes on more than sixty artists, with sources for each fact, see `library/INDEX.md` and `library/MOVES.md`.

- Hans Ulrich Obrist, do it, ongoing since 1993: an exhibition made only of artists' instructions that anyone can carry out. [checked]

## Forms to work in

Each of these is a different way for a work to exist. Rotate through them.

- **Instruction**: steps someone else can carry out (LeWitt wall drawings, Ono).
- **Event score**: a very short score for an action, often one line (Brecht, Fluxus).
- **Statement**: words presented as the work itself, which may or may not be built (Weiner).
- **Proposition**: a definition or claim about what art is (Kosuth, Art & Language).
- **Unrealisable proposal**: a work that cannot or should not be made (Barry, Ono).
- **Duration or documentation piece**: a rule plus the record it produces (Huebler, On Kawara).
- **Systems piece**: a set of rules that generates every variation (LeWitt, Bochner, Darboven).
- **Social or institutional piece**: a work that uses an audience or an institution as its material (Haacke, Piper).
- **Photo and text**: an image whose meaning comes from a caption or rule (Baldessari, Huebler).
- **Inventory or list**: counting, measuring or naming as the whole act (Bochner, Kawara).

- Sigmund Freud, "A Note upon the Mystic Writing-Pad", 1925: a toy pad of a wax slab under a thin covering sheet; lifting the sheet clears the writing, but the wax keeps every trace; Freud took it as a model of memory. Not an artist; cited as a thinker. [known]

- R. Murray Schafer, The Tuning of the World, 1977: coined the soundscape term keynote, the sound a place is pitched in, so constant it is heard as background and not noticed; the word borrowed from music, where the keynote is the home note of a key. Not an artist in the series' sense; cited as a composer and thinker. [known]
- Mains hum: alternating current in North America runs at 60 cycles a second, and the hum it sets up sits between B flat and B in standard concert tuning (A at 440). Cited as a fact. [known]
- Jorge Luis Borges, "On Exactitude in Science", 1946: a one paragraph fable of an empire whose cartographers made a map the size of the empire, point for point; later generations found it useless and left it to the weather, and its tattered ruins lie in the western deserts, sheltering animals and beggars. - Daniel Defoe, Robinson Crusoe, 1719: after years alone on the island, Crusoe finds the print of a single bare foot on the shore, only one and no others, and is terrified by it. Not an artist; cited as a novel. [known]
- John Locke, Second Treatise of Government, chapter 5, "Of Property" (published 1689, title page dated 1690): a person makes a thing their property by mixing their labour with it. Cited as a philosopher. [known]
- Aristotle, On Interpretation, chapter 9: whether the sentence "there will be a sea battle tomorrow" is true or false today; his answer is that statements about such future events are not yet either. Cited as a philosopher. [known]
- Robert FitzRoy, head of what became the Met Office, began publishing daily weather forecasts in The Times in August 1861. He called them opinions reached by scientific calculation, not prophecies, and was mocked in the press when they failed. Not an artist; cited as a historical figure. Met Office archive page on FitzRoy found by web search, Nov 2026. [checked]
