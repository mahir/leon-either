# No. 048 Cut From the Last One

## Image 1 (main shot)

```
Overhead photograph looking straight down at a long strip of clean white paper lying on a pale oak table, lit by soft morning daylight from a window on the left. On the paper lie exactly twenty brass house keys in two neat rows of ten, evenly spaced, all the same ordinary pin tumbler key with the same round bow, all lying flat with bows at the top and teeth pointing down and to the right. Every key has the same pattern of teeth. Going from the first key at top left to the last key at bottom right, the keys change very gradually: the first ones are bright new brass with sharp, crisp teeth; the last ones are a little duller and their teeth slightly softer and rounder. The change is subtle, never broken or bent. Each key is about 5 cm long. Crisp, natural contemporary colour photograph, documentary, not staged like an advert. Square format, 1:1. No people, no hands, no key rings, no tools, no logos, no brand names, no text or engraving on the keys, no other objects.
```

## Image 2

```
Close up photograph of one open adult palm, held flat and facing up, near a window in soft daylight. Resting side by side across the palm are exactly two brass house keys of the same shape and size, both lying flat with their teeth facing the same way. The upper key is bright, with sharp, clean, crisply cut teeth. The lower key has the very same pattern of teeth, the same number of peaks in the same places, but every peak is worn soft and rounded and the brass is a little duller. Both keys are about 5 cm long, small against the palm. Shallow depth of field, the background a soft blurred pale wall. Natural contemporary colour photograph, documentary. Square format, 1:1. No other keys, no key ring, no rings or jewellery, no logos, no brand names, no text or engraving on the keys.
```
